Tow math

The tow rating in the ad belongs to a truck with a driver, a full tank and nothing else. The number that actually runs out first is usually payload - and the trailer's tongue spends it before anyone climbs in. Ten to fifteen percent of the loaded trailer presses on the hitch, and that weight, plus people, plus cargo, has to fit inside the door sticker. Tow math does the arithmetic the brochure skips: trailer weight becomes honest tongue weight, the sticker becomes the real margin, and the brake question gets its labeled line. The three tools

  • Tongue plan - the loaded trailer becomes the 10-15% that presses on the hitch.
  • Payload plan - the sticker minus people, cargo and tongue becomes the margin, or the honest shortfall.
  • Brake plan - the trailer's weight meets the common 3,000 lb brake line, with the gray zone named.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: tongue percentages and brake lines are starting points, not rulings - brake laws differ by state and the manual's own limits can be stricter. Ratings are ceilings, not recommendations: the lowest number in the chain (truck, hitch, ball, coupler) is the limit. Distribute the load, check the lights, and a sway that starts is a reason to slow down gently, not to brake hard.
